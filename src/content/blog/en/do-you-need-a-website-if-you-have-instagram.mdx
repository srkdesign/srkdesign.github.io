## Short Answer

Yes. A social media page can help people discover your business, but it can't replace a website. You don't own your social media page, the platform does. A website is the one place online that's actually yours: it builds trust, shows up in search results, and can't be taken away by a policy change or moderation mistake.

## Why so many business owners think social media is enough

It's an easy trap to fall into. Setting up an Instagram or Facebook page is free and takes 10 minutes. You already have followers there. Posting feels easier than maintaining a website. For a while, it can genuinely work, especially for a very new business testing an idea.

The problem shows up later, once business depends on that page to be found, trusted, and reachable. That's when the gaps in social-only presence start to cost real money.

## 5 things a website does that social media can't

### 1. Website builds trust

Most people judge a business partly by whether it has a real website. In fact, 84% of consumers say a website makes a businesss feel more credible than a social media profile alone, according to research cited by Network Solutions. A separate 2026 survey from DreamHost found that 69% of cunsumers consider a website essential for local business to seem credible. A social page shows people you're active. A website shows people you're legitimate.

### 2. Website shows up when people search you

When someone searches Google or AI-agent for a business like yours, a website can appear in the results. A social media page rarely does. That means every consumer who searches instead of scrolling, which is the most people looking for a specific product or service, may never find a business that only exists on social media.

### 3. You don't own your social media page, the platform does

Every post, follower, and message onInstagram, Facebook, or any other platform lives on servers you don't control. The platform sets the rules, and it can change them at any time. Website, on the other hand, is something the business actually owns% the domain, the hosting, the content.

### 4. Platforms can and do remove business pages wihtout warning

This isn't a hypothetical risk. In 2025, an automated moderation error wiped out thousands of small business and community pages with no warning, including accounts belonging to photographers and beauticians who relied on them for bookings. Recovery was slow, and many appeals only succeeded after journalists got involved. Even a best-case recovery doesn't restore things instantly, reach typically has to rebuild from scratch, which can take weeks.

### 5. Website can do things a social profile simply can't

Booking systems built around your actual services. A full product catalog. Payment set up the way your business needs it. Custom pages for each service or location. None of this fits neatly into social media profile, which is built for short posts and quick browsing, not for running a business.

## Best of both worlds

None of this means social media isn't worth using. Social media is often where people discover a business in the first place, especially though recommendations, shares, and browsing. Website is where they go next, to check the business is real, learn more, and decide to actually reach out or buy.
Businesses that rely on only one tend to leave the other job undone, and lose customers at exactly the point they were ready to convert.

## Ask yourself this

If your social media page disappeared tomorrow, could customers still find your business, learn what you offer, and contact you?

If the answer is no, that's the gap worth closing, and it doesn't require abandoning social media, just adding the piece it can't provide.
